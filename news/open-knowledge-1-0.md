The first version of **Open Knowledge** is out — an open-source, MIT-licensed
application that turns curated knowledge into a course library anyone in the
world can study. No accounts required to learn. No personal data collected.
No platform in the middle.

What ships in this first version:

- **A public course catalog** with search, language and category filters,
  and cover-art-first course cards.
- **An immersive study mode**: careful long-form typography, Markdown, audio
  and video materials in a deliberate pedagogical order, visual progress,
  and continue-where-you-left-off — like a book that remembers your page.
- **Exams that teach**: explanatory feedback after every answer, and
  question pools that draw a random subset per attempt.
- **Thirteen interface languages**, with courses declaring their own
  language and the catalog filtering by it.
- **Two deployment modes**: a fully *static* mode where your content lives
  in a public git repository and the app is stateless (this very site runs
  that way), and a *database* mode with pseudonymous TOTP identities,
  cross-device progress, server-graded exams, shareable certificates with
  PDF download, a full visual admin panel, EPUB/PDF course export and
  one-click environment backups.
- **Mobile first**, light and dark themes, and an interface that gets out of
  the way of reading.

The two courses in this library tell the whole story: where the philosophy
comes from, and exactly how to run a library of your own.

Open Knowledge is a gift, not a business. If you have knowledge worth
sharing, [deploying your own library](https://github.com/oegea/open-knowledge)
takes a few minutes.
