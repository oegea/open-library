Open Knowledge is an open-source application (MIT license) that publishes a
**library of courses**: knowledge someone has curated, organized into a
deliberate learning path, and offered openly on the web.

For the **learner**, an Open Knowledge library feels like this:

- A **catalog** of courses with cover art, searchable and filterable by
  language and category. Thirteen interface languages.
- Every course can be **studied completely without registering** — in the
  spirit of a public library, or of audit mode in online education. Your
  progress is remembered by your own browser.
- A **study mode built for focus**: careful long-form typography, one
  material at a time, a clear sense of where you are and what comes next,
  and continue-where-you-left-off, like a book that remembers your page.
- Materials can be **text (Markdown), audio, video, or exams**. Exams give
  **explanatory feedback**: after answering you learn *why* the answer was
  right or wrong, not just a score. Question pools draw a random subset per
  attempt.
- Courses declare their **authorship, bibliography, content license** and —
  always disclosed — whether **AI assisted** in producing them.
- Mobile first, light and dark themes, EPUB and PDF export of whole courses.

For the **publisher** — you — Open Knowledge is a tool that takes a folder
of Markdown and JSON, or the contents of a small database you manage through
a friendly admin panel, and turns it into all of the above without further
effort. The interface, the reading experience, the exams, the translations:
already done. Your job is the part only you can do — **curating knowledge
worth sharing**.

One application, two very different ways to run it. That choice — **static
content mode** versus **database mode** — is the subject of the rest of this
course.
